# Hallux Rigidus Resources

Hallux rigidus is osteoarthritis of the first metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint—the joint at the base of the big toe. It can cause pain, stiffness, swelling, a bony prominence on top of the joint, and difficulty during the push-off phase of walking.

This repository is a small, non-commercial index of educational resources for people learning about the condition. It is not a software project and it does not provide individual medical advice.

## Practical topics to understand

### Symptoms and assessment

Common features include reduced upward movement of the big toe, pain during walking or running, tenderness around the joint, and discomfort from shoe pressure. Similar symptoms can arise from gout, bunions, turf toe and other conditions, so diagnosis should come from a qualified healthcare professional.

- [What is hallux rigidus?](https://halluxrigiduscare.com/what-is-hallux-rigidus/)
- [When to see a doctor for toe pain](https://halluxrigiduscare.com/when-to-see-a-doctor-for-toe-pain/)

### Footwear

Helpful footwear often combines a resistant forefoot, rocker-shaped sole, adequate toe-box width and height, stable cushioning, and a secure heel. Fit should be assessed with any orthotic or insert in place.

- [Hallux rigidus footwear guide](https://halluxrigiduscare.com/best-shoes-for-hallux-rigidus/)
- [Insoles and orthotics overview](https://halluxrigiduscare.com/best-insoles-for-hallux-rigidus/)

### Treatment discussions

Management depends on symptoms, activity goals, examination findings and arthritis severity. Conservative approaches may include activity modification, footwear changes, orthoses, medication guidance from a clinician, or injections in selected cases. Surgery may be discussed when persistent symptoms substantially limit daily life.

## Medical disclaimer

These links are for general education only. They do not replace diagnosis or treatment from a podiatrist, orthopaedic foot-and-ankle specialist, physiotherapist or another qualified healthcare professional. Seek urgent care for severe injury, sudden intense swelling, spreading redness, fever, or inability to bear weight.
